Labels used below:

- **[H] Hypothesis**: inferred, and needs validation with more coaches.

## Primary persona: young coach developing professionally (Segment A)

### Professional profile
- [O] Has coached professionally for about 1 to 2 years. Coaching is their main job.
- [O] Works mainly in person, with about 10 current athletes.
- [O] Athletes are mostly beginner and intermediate, of mixed ages and fitness levels.
- [O] Has experience in bodybuilding, several sports, and basic corrective exercises.
- [O] Gets athletes mostly through referrals and gym promotion.
- [O] Used to write programs on paper.
- [O] Wants to improve their skills and service quality. Wants to move to a higher-level gym.
- [O] Thinks social media matters for growth, but finds it hard to make time for it.
- [O] Had not paid for professional digital tools before.
- [O] Learns from colleagues and from credible Instagram and Telegram sources. Has a fairly small professional network and values peer learning.
- **Not established:** age, gender, income, city, personality, purchasing power. Don't assume them in copy.

### Practical problems
1. [O] Writing workout programs takes too long.
2. [O] Repeatedly explaining technique, execution details and common mistakes eats time.
3. [O] A good exercise library matters to them.
4. [O] Looking back at earlier programs when writing a new one is cumbersome.
5. [O] It's hard to remember which exercises were used in previous months.
6. [H] More athletes would increase the load of writing and tracking programs.
7. [O] Finding time for social content and business growth is hard.
8. [O] Professional connections and paths to career progression are limited.

### Value reported from Komak Morabi (one person's experience, not a promise)
- [O] Writing programs became easier and faster. No measurement was taken, so never quote a percentage or a number of minutes.
- [O] They could deliver a program on the same day the athlete registered.
- [O] Their workflow became more structured.
- [O] The exercise library is especially valuable. They add exercises and demonstration clips themselves.
- [O] Seeing the team act on user feedback increased their trust.
- [O] They have recommended the app to coaching friends.

### Product feedback
- [O] Suggestion: when writing a new program, visibly mark exercises from this athlete's previous programs. The point is not to block reuse, but to recognise reuse quickly and decide.
- Status: a matching marker exists in the code. See `product-claims.md` ⚠️ "Already used for this athlete" marker. Confirm it's live, then validate with other coaches whether it helps.

### Motivations ([H] unless noted, so explore them in content and research)
- Becoming a more capable, confident coach. ([O] wants to improve skills.)
- Giving athletes a better service.
- Preparing programs efficiently. ([O])
- Organising professional knowledge and demonstrations. ([O] library.)
- Building a reputation and growing the business.
- Learning from experienced colleagues. ([O])
- Practical tools that don't add complexity.

### Barriers (discover which matter. Don't assume all apply.)
- [O] Limited time.
- [O] No prior paid tools, so willingness to pay is unknown.
- [O] A habit of paper-based workflows.
- [O] A limited network.
- [H] Low familiarity with digital business tools.
- [H] Fear of learning yet another app.
- [H] Uncertainty about what a tool improves day to day.
- [H] Informal workflows in messaging apps such as Telegram, WhatsApp and Eitaa.

### Core insight [H, strongest working hypothesis]
This coach isn't looking for more software. They want to work efficiently, keep professional standards, grow, and progress in a competitive field.

Content should **show a practical tool reducing everyday friction while the coach keeps their expertise and control.**

## Working segments (not validated, not sized)

| Segment | Who | Content leans on | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A: Early-career** (default) | Building experience and confidence, small athlete base | Practical education, simple workflows, peer learning | Likely price-sensitive. Don't be condescending. |
| **B: Busy** | Many athletes, repetitive programming and admin | Templates, the auto-saved draft, athlete history, consistency | Skip basics. Show scale and control. |
| **C: Building online presence** | Wants athletes from social media | Professional identity, content ideas, online requests, public listing (Pro) | Don't promise follower or income growth. |
| **D: Established** | Experienced, set workflow, sceptical | Concrete workflow evidence, control, exporting their own expertise | No hype. Show real screens. Respect their process. |

When no segment is given, write for A and the needs it shares with B: time spent writing programs, reuse, and organisation.
